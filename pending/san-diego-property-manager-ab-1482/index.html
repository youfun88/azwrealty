Finding a San Diego Property Manager Who Knows AB 1482

What AB 1482 and San Diego’s tenant protections require, which rentals are exempt, and the questions that show whether a manager knows them.

Updated November 14, 2026

Short answer

How do I find a San Diego property manager who knows AB 1482 and California tenant law?

Ask each manager to explain, for your property, whether the state rent cap applies, which exemption notice (if any) belongs in the lease, and what a no-fault termination would cost in the City of San Diego. A manager who knows AB 1482 (the Tenant Protection Act of 2019, Civil Code §§ 1946.2 and 1947.12) and the city’s Residential Tenant Protections ordinance can answer clearly and point to the source. What is AB 1482?

AB 1482 is the Tenant Protection Act of 2019. It added two rules to the California Civil Code (as summarized in the DRE 2026 guide):

  • A rent cap (§ 1947.12): for covered rentals, gross rent increases in any 12-month period are capped at 5% plus the change in the cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower, with no more than two increases in that period. You can set the starting rent at any amount; the cap applies to increases after that. The law expires on January 1, 2030, unless extended.
  • Just cause (§ 1946.2): once a tenancy has been in place for 12 months, a covered tenancy can end only for an at-fault reason (such as unpaid rent or a material lease breach) or a no-fault reason (such as the owner moving in, taking the unit off the market, or a substantial remodel). A no-fault termination under state law requires one month’s rent in relocation assistance, paid or waived.

Does AB 1482 apply to my San Diego rental?

Most rentals are covered, but the rent cap does not apply to these, among others:

  • Housing less than 15 years old.
  • A duplex where the owner lived in one unit when the tenancy began and still lives there.
  • A single-family home or condo whose owner is not a real estate investment trust, a corporation, or an LLC with at least one corporate member, if the owner gave the tenant written notice that the unit is exempt.

That last exemption depends on the notice: the owner must give it for the home to be exempt. A manager who knows the law will ask who owns the property before the first lease is written.

How do San Diego’s own rules go further than AB 1482?

In the City of San Diego, the Residential Tenant Protections ordinance (San Diego Municipal Code §§ 98.0701–98.0710) adds to state law:

  • Just cause from the start: the city’s definition of a tenancy covers a right to occupy for more than 30 days, and leaves out fixed-term leases of three months or less. State law alone generally starts after 12 months.
  • More relocation assistance: a no-fault termination requires two months of the tenant’s actual rent, or three months if the tenant is 62 or older or disabled (§ 98.0706(c)). State law alone requires one month.
  • Exact wording: the city’s exemption for a separately sellable home requires the exact statement from the code, and for a tenancy started or renewed on or after January 1, 2024, the statement must be in the lease (§ 98.0703).
  • Notices: covered rentals need the city’s tenant protections notice and the San Diego Housing Commission’s Tenant Protection Guide (§ 98.0705).
  • Penalties: failing to strictly follow § 98.0706 voids the termination notice, and failing to pay required relocation assistance makes the landlord liable for at least three times that amount plus actual economic damages (§ 98.0709).

If your rental is in another city in San Diego County, state law still applies; check whether that city has its own rules. Our San Diego landlord rules guide covers rent increase notices, deposits, and owner move-in in more detail.

What should I ask a property manager about AB 1482?

Good answers are specific to your property and name the rule they come from.

  • “Is my property covered by the state rent cap?” Listen for questions about the building’s age, who owns it (a person, a trust, an LLC, a corporation), and whether an exemption notice was given.
  • “How do you set a rent increase on a covered unit?” The answer should mention the 12-month cap, the two-increase limit, and written notice.
  • “What goes into the lease for a San Diego rental?” Listen for the city’s tenant protections notice, the Tenant Protection Guide, and any exemption statement in the exact wording the code requires.
  • “If I need my home back, what does it cost?” In the City of San Diego, expect relocation assistance of two or three months’ rent and conditions on owner move-in. See renting out your San Diego home after you move.
  • “How do you handle late rent and lease violations?” A curable violation needs written notice and a chance to fix it first.
  • “Who is your broker, and what is the license number?” Check it on the DRE license lookup.

Frequently asked questions

What is AB 1482?

AB 1482 is the Tenant Protection Act of 2019. It caps rent increases on covered California rentals (Civil Code § 1947.12) and requires just cause to end certain tenancies (Civil Code § 1946.2).

How much can rent go up under AB 1482?

For covered rentals, gross rent increases in any 12-month period are capped at 5% plus the change in the cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower, with no more than two increases in that period.

Is a single-family home exempt from AB 1482?

It can be. A single-family home or condo whose owner is not a real estate investment trust, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member is exempt from the rent cap if the owner gave the tenant written notice that the unit is exempt.

Does San Diego have stricter rules than AB 1482?

Yes, in the City of San Diego. Just cause can apply from the start of a tenancy of more than 30 days (fixed-term leases of three months or less are left out), and a no-fault termination requires two months’ rent in relocation assistance, or three months for a tenant who is 62 or older or disabled.
