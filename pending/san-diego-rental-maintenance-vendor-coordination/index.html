How San Diego Property Managers Handle Maintenance and Vendors

What should happen between a tenant’s repair request and a finished job, what California requires, and how to compare how managers handle it.

Updated November 14, 2026

Short answer

How should a San Diego property management company handle maintenance requests and vendors?

It should answer every request within a stated time, treat health and safety problems as urgent, send a vetted vendor with proper notice to the tenant, and keep a record of the job and the cost. Ask each manager for its response times in writing and whether it adds a markup to repair bills. Azure Waves Realty responds to urgent maintenance issues within 24 hours and non-urgent ones within 48 hours, has a team ready for emergencies 24/7, uses vetted vendors, and adds no markup: you pay the repair cost.

From request to repair · Vendors · Questions to ask

What should happen when a tenant reports a repair?

  1. The request is answered. A good manager has a response time it commits to. Ours, in our written promise: within 24 hours for urgent issues and within 48 hours for non-urgent ones.
  2. It is sorted by urgency. Problems that affect health and safety come first (see the next section).
  3. A vendor is sent. The right trade for the job: plumber, electrician, contractor, gardener, or handyman.
  4. The tenant gets notice of entry. California requires reasonable advance written notice, stating the date, approximate time, and purpose. The law considers 24 hours’ written notice reasonable in most situations. No advance notice is needed in an emergency.
  5. The work is recorded. A dated record of each request and repair protects you if there is ever a dispute.
  6. You see the cost. As an owner with us, you get a detailed monthly statement and an annual summary.

Which rental repairs are urgent in California?

A landlord must keep a rental fit to live in. The DRE 2026 guide gives examples of serious defects directly related to health and safety, such as a roof leak in the rainy season, no hot running water, or a gas leak. A home may also be considered uninhabitable if it substantially lacks working plumbing, gas, heating, or electrical systems. Since January 1, 2026, a working stove and refrigerator are part of a habitable home, unless the landlord and tenant agree the tenant provides them.

Delays have consequences. For a serious defect, a tenant who has told the landlord and waited a reasonable time may be able to have the repair done and deduct its cost from the rent, up to one month’s rent, no more than twice in 12 months. See who pays for repairs on a San Diego rental for the full rules.

How does a property manager choose and oversee vendors?

Ask how vendors are picked, how long the manager has worked with them, and who is responsible if the work is not right. Here is how we do it at Azure Waves Realty (Maintenance & Service):

  • Vetted vendors: plumbers, electricians, contractors, gardeners, and handymen, many of whom have worked with us for years.
  • Guaranteed work: their work is guaranteed and warrantied, and they go back to fix issues you are unhappy with.
  • Discounts passed on: our vendors give us discounts, and we pass them on to our clients.
  • Your vendor is welcome: if you have a preferred vendor, we are happy to work with them.

Do property managers mark up repair bills?

Some do, so ask. A markup or a maintenance coordination fee raises the cost of every repair. Azure Waves Realty adds no maintenance markup: you pay the repair cost, with nothing added by us. Maintenance is part of Full-Service Management at 8% of collected monthly rent ($180 minimum per unit), with no management fee during a vacancy or eviction. See every fee explained.

How does a manager keep repairs from growing?

  • Move-in and move-out records: we document both in writing and with photos.
  • Yearly walk-throughs: we walk through your home each year to help protect your investment.
  • Tenant duties: California law also requires tenants to use plumbing, gas, and electrical fixtures properly and to tell the landlord when dead bolts, window locks, or smoke or carbon monoxide detectors do not work.

What should I ask a property manager about maintenance?

  • What are your response times for urgent and non-urgent requests, and are they in writing?
  • Who answers emergencies at night and on weekends?
  • Do you add a markup or a coordination fee to repair bills?
  • Up to what amount do you approve a repair without calling me first?
  • Can I use my own vendor?
  • How will I see what was repaired and what it cost?
