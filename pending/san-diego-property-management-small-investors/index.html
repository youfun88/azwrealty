Property Management for Small Investors with 1 to 5 San Diego Rentals

How to compare property managers when you own a few units, what it costs per unit, and the California and San Diego rules small landlords run into most.

Updated November 9, 2026

Short answer

How should a small investor with one to five San Diego rentals choose a property manager?

Instead of relying on a “best of” list, compare managers on terms you can get in writing: how the fee works per unit, upfront costs, fees while a unit is vacant, how easily you can leave, how tenants are screened, and when you get paid. Then check the license with the California Department of Real Estate. Azure Waves Realty manages single-family homes, condos, and multifamily properties across San Diego County for 8% of collected monthly rent ($180 minimum per unit), with $0 upfront and a multi-property discount quote when you have more than one property with us.

Why does a small portfolio need different questions?

With one to five units, each vacancy, each bad placement, and each minimum fee is a large share of your income. A large firm’s terms can look fine on one property and add up quickly across three. So ask how every fee applies per unit, and whether anything changes when you add or remove a property.

How much does property management cost for one to five units in San Diego?

With Azure Waves Realty, each unit is billed on its own collected rent. Full-Service Management is 8% of collected monthly rent, with a $180 minimum per unit in months when rent is collected. The minimum applies when monthly rent is below $2,250, because 8% of $2,250 is $180. A unit with no rent coming in because of a vacancy or an eviction pays no management fee.

Example with three units:

  • Unit A rents for $3,000. The 8% fee is $240.
  • Unit B rents for $2,000. 8% would be $160, so the fee is $180, because the Full-Service Management minimum is $180 per unit.
  • Unit C rents for $2,500. The 8% fee is $200.
  • Total: $7,500 in rent and $620 a month in management fees.

When a new tenant is placed, a one-time placement fee of 25% of one month’s rent applies, with an $899 minimum, taken from the first month’s collected rent. There is no lease renewal fee and no markup on maintenance. If you have more than one property with us, ask for a multi-property discount quote. Should a small investor choose full service or tenant placement only?

  • Tenant Placement Only: one month’s rent ($750 minimum), with no monthly fee. It includes a rent assessment, marketing, professional photos, in-person showings, and tenant screening. You then collect rent, handle repairs, and keep the records yourself.
  • Full-Service Management: everything above, plus rent collection, maintenance with 24/7 emergency service, move-in and move-out documentation with photos, yearly walk-throughs, and monthly statements and an annual summary.

Placement only can suit an owner who lives nearby and has time for repairs and bookkeeping. Full service tends to make more sense when you live far away, own several units, or would rather not take tenant calls. If you start with placement and later want full service, our team is ready to support you. Our guide on hiring a manager or self-managing lists the work you take on yourself.

Which California and San Diego rules matter most to a landlord with a few rentals?

  • Security deposits: generally no more than one month’s rent. Small landlords, meaning individuals or LLCs owned by people that own and rent one or two properties with no more than 4 rental units, can charge up to two months’ rent, but only one month’s rent to a service member (Civil Code § 1950.5, as summarized in the DRE 2026 guide).
  • Rent increase cap: a single-family home or condo whose owner is not a real estate investment trust, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member is exempt from the state cap, if the owner gave the tenant written notice that the unit is exempt (Civil Code § 1947.12, as summarized in the DRE 2026 guide). Covered rentals are capped.
  • Just cause in the City of San Diego: a landlord of a covered rental needs just cause to end a tenancy. One exemption covers a home that can be sold separately when the owner is not a real estate investment trust, a corporation, or an LLC with a corporate member. It requires the exact statement from the code, and for a tenancy started or renewed on or after January 1, 2024, the statement must be in the lease (San Diego Municipal Code § 98.0703).
  • City rental tax: the Rental Unit Business Tax is imposed annually on anyone who owns, operates, or manages the rental of residential real estate in the City of San Diego.

The San Diego landlord rules guide covers each of these in more detail, with the sources.
